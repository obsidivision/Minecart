How it works

A track is one straight lane of rails. You place a minecart on the first rail, it rolls along, and it stops on the last rail: a powered rail that is off, sloping up. Where it stops on that slope sets its height: the y fraction is 1/16 plus how far up the slope it got. The finder tries every track it is allowed to build, in order, and keeps the ones whose cart ends closest to your target, or anywhere inside your range.

  • Exact physics. Every tick uses a JavaScript port of the Python simulator that reproduces FarCoolHat's stored cart bit for bit, and whose 13 floatcart makers were confirmed in game, every coordinate to the last digit. The port was checked against it on 7,980 full runs, all identical to the last bit.
  • x or z values. Once the cart has parked it has no speed at all, so if you then break the parking rail it falls straight down: its x and z stay exactly what they were, and it lands on the block below. That position along the track is what an x or z target aims for (x for a track running east or west, z for north or south). Checked with the simulator on 2,100 drops. A drop of less than about 0.0003 blocks never happens (rule C4), so a cart parked just above a block top stays hovering there.
  • Fast. A partly built track is simulated once and shared by every track that continues it, so each rail added costs only the ticks the cart spends on it. The work is split across your processor's cores. Nothing leaves your browser.
  • Buildable. Glass under every rail, and at the foot of each slope's high side. No peak: a slope up is never followed straight away by a slope down. Powered rails that are on and off never touch, since touching powered rails share power; each run of powered rails that are on gets a lever on a concrete block beside its first rail.
  • Downloads. Download .litematic saves the track alone. Download with tester adds command blocks that test it by themselves: paste it in a creative world with commands allowed, and a few seconds later they put a minecart on the start rail; once it has parked (and, for an x or z target, once they have removed the parking rail) they say in chat whether it passed, with its exact position, and put a lime or red wool block above it. A button behind the start runs the test again. They are built from the same parts as the 13-maker test rig, which worked in game.
  • Where you build matters. The cart's position is a floating-point number, so its last digits depend on where the track is. Results are exact at the build position you set. Check at 60 positions reruns a track at 15 places in the world, out to the world border, in all 4 directions (in your direction only for an x or z target).
  • Nothing else may touch the cart. Every result is for a plain, empty minecart placed by hand on the start rail, with any levers already on. A minecart moving at 0.1 blocks per tick or faster picks up mobs (and other entities it can push) that come within 0.2 blocks of it, and any passenger changes its physics: it moves only 75% of its speed each tick and keeps 99.7% of it instead of 96%, so it stops somewhere else entirely. Players, iron golems and other minecarts are pushed instead, and that pushes the cart too. Keep the track clear, and don't let anything bump the cart until you are done with it. The experimental minecart changes must be off (the default).
